## Text

> **Transient 503s from `llm-clef-flash` — measured rate, and a question about expected behaviour**
>
> While measuring something else (whether two concurrent calls are served in parallel — they are, the
> endpoint does not appear to serialise same-origin requests), I counted HTTP 503s on the
> `:predict` endpoint over 2026-10-14:
>
> - **~3.5 % of first attempts: 4 of ~116 calls** (Wilson 95 % interval **1.4 %–8.5 %** — a small
>   sample, so please read it as "a few percent", not as 3.5), all made from *inside* the Toolforge
>   pod (WMCS origin, the known-network tier), so no anonymous-tier 429s are mixed into this figure.
> - The body is the Envoy connection-level failure, not a model error:
>   `{"httpCode":503,"httpReason":"upstream connect error or disconnect/reset before headers. reset
>   reason: connection timeout"}`. My reading is that the request never reached a model — consistent
>   with `usage` never being returned for these and no tokens being spent.
> - **No `Retry-After`** and no `x-envoy-ratelimited` on any of them; they are not rate-limit
>   responses.
> - The endpoint was otherwise **healthy** in the same sessions: median 0.33 s/call, HTTP 200 for
>   everything else, no 429s at any point in the day.
> - They were **isolated**, not a burst: failures appeared at different positions in a sequence with
>   successful calls immediately before and after (e.g. one at the very first call of a run, one as
>   the last call of a serial phase, two at position 2 in separate phases). I did not see two failures
>   adjacent in time.
>
> **Questions, if you have a moment:**
>
> 1. Is a low rate of `upstream connect error … connection timeout` (as opposed to a 5xx from the
>    model itself) a known characteristic of the current single-replica serving setup — backend pod
>    churn, connection-pool behaviour at the gateway — or something you would want to look at?
> 2. **Is a retry expected to succeed?** I have not been able to answer that from my side, which is a
>    gap worth naming: I have the *rate* but not the *recovery* rate, and I do not want to buy that
>    number by firing a few hundred extra calls at a service that is still being adjusted. If a short
>    retry is normally enough, that is useful to know; if it usually is not, that changes what a client
>    should do.
> 3. Is there any signal a client could use to distinguish "the backend is briefly unconnectable" from
>    "the service is down" (a health/status endpoint, or a header)? At the moment a client can only
>    react to individual failures.
>
> **Context for the numbers, so they can be judged:** this is the WikiPortraits evaluation tool
> (<https://wikiportraits-eval.toolforge.org>), which is probably the heaviest consumer of this
> endpoint — it scores one photo per call at ~1,000–1,300 input tokens and generates no output. Its
> client side currently treats this as a transient fault: up to **3 attempts** per photo with
> exponential backoff (2 s → 4 s), every attempt paced through the same global start-rate gate as a
> first attempt, and the whole run **stops** after 5 consecutive photos fail this way so that an
> outage cannot be multiplied across a queue. Each run now also reports how many photos were retried
> and how many recovered, so the recovery rate will accumulate from ordinary use rather than from a
> dedicated load test — I can report that back here once there is something to report.
>
> Reproduction, if useful: the calls are plain
> `POST https://api.wikimedia.org/service/lw/inference/v1/models/llm-clef-flash:predict` with the
> six-question body and a base64 thumbnail, made from `kubectl exec` inside the deployment. The
> measurement scripts are in the repo (`tools/probe_concurrency.py`, and `tools/run_in_pod.sh` to run from the
> pod); the write-up is `research/concurrency-2026-10-14.md`.

## Notes for the person posting (not part of the comment)

- **Where each number came from.** 4 failures / ~116 calls is a tally across several probe runs in the
  pod on 2026-10-14 (`research/concurrency-pod-2streams-n12-2026-10-14.txt`,
  `research/concurrency-pod-4streams-n8-2026-10-14.txt`, and two runs whose output was read from the
  console). The per-call timings come from the same files. The 0.33 s median is n=6 in the pod.
- **What is *not* claimed.** No recovery rate (never measured — see the comment). No causal mechanism
  for the 503s. No claim that they correlate with concurrency: the failures appeared in *serial*
  phases, and the concurrent phases in the same runs had none, but n is far too small to make that a
  finding — say so rather than let a reader infer it.
- **The 3.5 % figure is a small sample — quote the interval, don't hide it.** Four events in 116
  calls. Wilson 95 % interval: **1.35 %–8.53 %** (half-width 3.59 percentage points). This was
  computed, not guessed (`python3 -c` with the Wilson formula); an earlier draft of this note carried
  a made-up ±1.7 %, which is a good illustration of why the number should be derived before it is
  written down. Saying "about 3.5 %, on a small sample" is the honest phrasing.
- **Do not include** the raw User-Agent, IPs, or any Toolforge request-log content — that is the
  platform's log and contains client identifiers (see `HANDOFF.md` §13).
